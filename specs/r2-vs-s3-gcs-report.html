Cost research memo

Does R2's free egress actually beat S3 and GCS for this workload?

A 200GB personal audiobook/e-book library, ~3 devices repeatedly downloading 60–100GB/month. Verified against primary pricing pages, July 2026.

Scope: R2 · AWS S3 · GCP Cloud Storage Workload: 200GB stored, 60–100GB/mo egress
Short answer

R2 is cheapest in every reading of the data — but not for the reason the hypothesis proposed. Against GCS, free egress really is the deciding factor (GCS's internet-egress pricing is high and its free allowance likely doesn't apply here). Against S3, egress is not the deciding factor at this volume — AWS gives 100GB/month of free internet egress, account-wide, which comfortably covers this workload. R2's edge over S3 is smaller, and comes from storage price plus unlimited egress headroom, not from S3 having an egress problem it doesn't have yet.

01AWS S3 — free tier and standard pricing

AWS restructured its free tier for new accounts on July 15, 2025 — later than the 2024 date recalled in the brief. New accounts now get a $100 credit on signup, up to $200 total via five $20 credit-earning activities (launch EC2, configure RDS, build a Lambda function, use Bedrock, set an AWS Budget). The free-plan window closes after 6 months or when credit runs out; unused credit expires at 12 months. This is generic spendable credit, not an S3-specific allowance — S3 is simply one eligible service to spend it on.

Flag — AWS's own pages disagree with each other

The current S3 pricing page reflects the new $100–200 credit model, but the S3 FAQ page still states verbatim: "new AWS customers receive 5 GB of Amazon S3 Standard storage, 20,000 Get Requests, 2,000 Put Requests, and 100GB of data transfer out each month for one year." That page appears stale post-restructuring. Either way, this legacy allowance was always time-limited ("for one year"), never perpetual — S3 has no line in AWS's genuinely-perpetual "Always Free" category. For an existing account with no credit left, standard rates apply from GB 1.

The pivotal fact

The 100GB free egress allowance is confirmed account-wide, aggregated across all AWS services and regions (excluding China/GovCloud) — identical wording appears on both the S3 pricing page and the EC2 on-demand pricing page. It is not 100GB per service. At 60–100GB/month of egress, this workload sits at or under that shared ceiling — meaning S3 egress cost here rounds to roughly $0, provided no other AWS service is drawing from the same pool.

Flag — could not fully verify against a primary source

The free allowance's exact wording is "100GB from North America to each Google Cloud Data transfer destination (Australia and China excluded)" — not "to the internet." The paid pricing table this would need to offset (id #network-egress, labelled "General network usage," defined as traffic "out of Google Cloud or between continents" — i.e. downloads to a phone or Mac) shows $0.12/GiB starting at 0GiB, with no cross-reference to the free-tier row. These are two structurally separate tables in Google's own pricing page with no stated link between them. No independent free-egress tracker (e.g. Cherry Servers' roundup of providers with free egress) lists GCP. Google support forum threads confirm users have been billed for egress while nominally "on the free tier." Six searches across GCP's own docs, SKU pages, and third-party analyses could not produce an explicit sentence resolving whether the 100GB line covers public-internet egress or only GCP-internal destinations. Treat this as unresolved — the cost table below shows both readings.

05Verdict

vs GCP Cloud Storage

Hypothesis confirmed. Egress is very likely the deciding factor. GCS's internet-egress rate ($0.12/GB) is higher than AWS's, its Always Free tier's 100GB data-transfer line most plausibly does not cover public-internet egress, and even its storage-only cost is close to R2's. At this workload GCS likely costs 3–5× what R2 costs, almost entirely on the egress line.

vs AWS S3

Hypothesis not confirmed as stated. AWS's 100GB/month free internet egress is real, current, and comfortably covers 60–100GB of monthly downloads. Egress is not what makes R2 cheaper here — R2's edge is its lower storage rate plus genuinely unlimited egress headroom, versus a free allowance that is shared across every other AWS service on the account and disappears entirely above 100GB/month.

Where would S3 or GCS actually win?

  • A library small enough to fit inside the free storage tier — under roughly 5–10GB with low, occasional egress. Neither applies here: 200GB blows past every provider's free storage allowance, and re-downloads after cache eviction keep egress a recurring cost, not a one-time event.
  • Cold, rarely-accessed archival storage — data pushed to S3 Glacier or GCS Coldline/Archive tiers (storage around $0.004/GB-month, egress rare enough that the per-GB rate barely matters). This app's actual pattern — three devices repeatedly re-fetching multi-GB files — is the opposite of that access pattern.

Research method: 5 parallel search agents against primary pricing pages (developers.cloudflare.com, aws.amazon.com, cloud.google.com), cross-checked against AWS/GCP blog and forum sources where pricing pages were ambiguous or JS-rendered. GCS's internet-egress free-tier scope remains flagged as unresolved against a primary source despite six follow-up queries — see §2.
